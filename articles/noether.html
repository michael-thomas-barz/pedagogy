Mechanics II: symmetry is conservation

July 10, 2026

Conservation laws are very important in physics. Let's review three key examples.

Conservation of energy

Let's imagine I let go of a ball of mass \(m\) at height \(h\). Initially, since the ball is not moving, there is no kinetic energy. However, there is a potential energy of \(mgh\) coming from gravity.

After some time \(t\), the ball has fallen to some height \(h'\). Since the gravitational acceleration is \(g\), the ball is now falling at speed \(gt\). So now the ball has kinetic energy of \[ \frac12 mv^2=\frac12m(gt)^2, \] and has potential energy \(mgh'\). By conservation of energy, we know \[ mgh=\frac12m(gt)^2+mgh', \] and we can solve for \(h'\): \[ h'=h-\frac12gt^2. \] Of course, if you wanted to solve this particular problem, you could have also done a little calculus. But conservation of energy alone can get you pretty far!

Conservation of momentum

Now, imagine I am playing billiard. A billiard ball of mass \(m_1\) is sitting on the table, and I hit it with another billiard ball of \(m_2\), moving at velocity \(v\). What happens to the balls?

Before the balls collide, my ball has mass \(m_2\) and velocity \(v\) so the momentum is \(m_2v\). Since the other billiard ball is just sitting, it has no momentum.

After the collision, the billiard ball I hit is moving at another velocity \(v_2\), and the billiard ball which was sitting on the table is now moving at some velocity \(v_1\). The total momentum here is \(m_1v_1+m_2v_2\).

By conservation of momentum, we know the total momentum is conserved, so \[ m_2v=m_1v_1+m_2v_2. \] If you combine this with the conservation of energy, \[ \frac12m_2v^2=\frac12m_1v_1^2+\frac12m_2v_2^2, \] you can solve for \(v_1\) and \(v_2\)!

Conservation of angular momentum

Angular momentum is defined with respect to some origin. For a particle with momentum \(\vec p\) and position vector \(\vec r\), the angular momentum is the cross product \(\vec r\times\vec p\).

For example, say there is a particle of mass \(m\) rotating in a circle of radius \(r\) around a rod with frequency \(f\). Then the particle is moving with velocity \(2\pi fr\), so the momentum is \(2\pi mfr\). Therefore, the angular momentum has size \[2\pi mfr^2.\] Let's say the particle moves into a smaller orbit, of radius \(r'<{r}\). That particle will have a new frequency \(f'\). Then conservation of angular momentum tells us \[2\pi mfr^2=2\pi mf'(r')^2.\] We can solve for \(f'\) as \[f'=\frac{r^2}{(r')^2}f>f.\] In other words, as the radius decreases, the frequency increases! This is the reason why when figure skaters pull in their arms, they rotate more frequently.

We see that each of our conserved quantities (energy, momentum, and angular momentum) tells us something useful about our physical system. However, finding these quantities requires some cleverness on our part. Wouldn't it be wonderful if there is a more systematic method for finding conserved quantities? Emmy Noether discovered that conserved quantities exactly correspond to continuous symmetries of the physical system, thereby answering this question!

What is continuous symmetry?

A windmill as below has symmetries. For example, rotating the picture by \(90^\circ\) doesn't change the picture. Neither does rotation by \(180^\circ\) or \(270^\circ\). Of course rotating by \(0^\circ\), which does nothing, also doesn't change the shape! However, if you rotate by, say, \(30^\circ\), the blades will be at a different position. So we would say the windmill has 4 symmetries: rotation by \(0^\circ\), \(90^\circ\), \(180^\circ\), and \(270^\circ\).

On the other hand, consider a circle.

Then, rotating by any angle \(\theta\) doesn't change the shape. That means the circle has infinitely many symmetries! That's why we would say a circle has a continuous set of symmetries, while the windmill only has a discrete set of symmetries.

Our world has three types of continuous symmertries:

  • Time symmetry: our universe will have the same physical laws 10 seconds from now.
  • Translation symmetry: the laws of physics on my block is the same as the laws of physics 3 blocks down.
  • Rotational symmetry: if I turn left, the laws of physics don't change.
Noether tells us that each of these symmetries corresponds to a conserved quantity.
